## The idea

Shilp is a Sanskrit word for craft, the kind you make with your hands. I borrowed it for an agency that doesn't exist. At the time my work was a loose pile of screens with no through-line, and I wanted it framed the way a studio frames things: a name, a mark, a site that says who does the work and why.

So I made one up. Some of what sits inside is real, like Sitenote, an app I'd already shipped. Some of it is concept, like a pitch deck and an expiry-tracker I designed to fill out the roster. All of it lives under one roof, which was the point.

## The identity

The mark is the word set in a clean grotesque, with the little ® that studios like to wear. Under it sits शिल्प, the Devanagari the name comes from. I put that on the launch posts so the meaning wasn't only there for people who'd go and look it up.

Black, white, one warm accent, and a lot of room to breathe. I wanted it to read as precise without straining for it.

## The site

A dark, grid-led marketing site. The hero says the plain thing out loud, that we design and build digital products, and the rest walks through what the studio does, the stack it uses, and the work.

I kept the grid visible on purpose. Rules run between the columns and everything hangs off them, which is what lets a dense technologies section and a near-empty project card still feel like the same place. It also saved me from styling every section on its own terms.

Almost everyone would land on a phone, so I drew mobile first and let the desktop follow. The same grid folds down to one column without losing its shape.

## The full build

A case study shows the tidy version. This is the Figma canvas behind it: every page and breakpoint I drew, from home and projects through the individual case pages, the journal and contact, on desktop and mobile both.

<Lede>Handing a design to a developer is where I found out which of my ideas were cheap and which cost a week.</Lede>

That changed how I draw. A layout that behaves in Figma can put up a fight in the browser, and the cost of a decision isn't visible on the canvas where you make it. Since Shilp I sketch the awkward states early and ask what something costs before I fall for it.

This is early work and it shows. The copy oversells the way agency copy always does, and dressing up solo work as a studio is a stretch I wouldn't make the same way now. Still, it was the first time I designed a whole site front to back and handed it to someone else to build. Shilp never took a client. It was the project that got me comfortable calling the work a practice.
