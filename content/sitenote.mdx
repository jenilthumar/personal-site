## Problem Discovery

Real estate and construction still run on WhatsApp threads and phone calls. Put enough people and departments on one project and that stops scaling. Messages get buried, and nobody's quite sure who said what.

And there's nowhere central to actually look. Records live buried in old chat threads. The sales pipeline is more or less invisible, and the office stays a step behind whatever's happening on site.

## Research & Scope

Research started with in-person interviews. Growing up around this business gave me a head start, and I kept talking to stakeholders the whole way through to keep the design honest about how the work actually happens.

Sitenote took shape as five core features, surfaced as channels. Not everyone needs all of them, and plenty of site workers have never touched enterprise software, so I built three roles, each with its own view and permissions. The interface leans on Material Design 3, since the audience already knows that language from WhatsApp.

## Final Thoughts

Sitenote launched on the web in April 2024. Two companies run on it daily now: the Sangani group in construction and the Opera group in real estate. The feedback's been good.

I built Sitenote as the founder and its first user, which taught me more than any brief could have. The daily design demos and weekly stakeholder meetings did the most for the quality. They kept me close to what clients actually needed, and to the engineering constraints behind every decision. If I did it again, I'd lock the core booking flow first, before letting the secondary features pull focus.
