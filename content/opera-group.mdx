## The brief

Opera Group builds across Navsari, in Gujarat, and the name carries weight locally after nearly three decades under the Sangani Group. Online, none of that came through. Someone who had heard about a project had nowhere to go that matched the buildings going up on the ground.

A home here is the biggest decision most families make. The site had to earn that trust before anyone picked up the phone. So the brief was short: make Opera feel as solid on a screen as it does at the site office.

## Designing for the decision

I put the projects first. Each one leads with a full render, then the details a buyer actually weighs: the layout, the size, and the handover date. That information sits where people look for it instead of buried in a brochure they have to download. A warm, serif-led identity does the rest, closer to a brand than a listings portal.

Almost everyone visits from a phone, so I built the mobile screens first and let desktop follow. Enquiries run through WhatsApp, because that is already where these conversations happen here. "Enquire now" and "Book a site visit" open a chat instead of a form nobody checks.

## The full build

A case study usually shows the highlight reel. This is the Figma canvas behind it: every page and state I drew for Opera. Home variations, the project listing and detail, the location map, the about pages, all of it on desktop and mobile. Working in one canvas is what kept the type and spacing consistent from screen to screen, so the site still feels like one place once you start clicking around.

<Full src="work/opera-group/08-figma-wireframes.webp" alt="Figma canvas of every Opera Group screen: home variations, project listing and detail, location map, and about pages, across desktop and mobile" />

## How it landed

The site is live at operagroup.co.in. The sales team has something that matches how they talk about the company in person, and a buyer can size up Opera before the first call instead of after it.

This was a change of pace from the product work I usually do. No dashboards, no roles, just one job: make a real estate company feel real on a screen. The part I would revisit is construction progress, since that is what buyers worry about most and the site only hints at it for now.
