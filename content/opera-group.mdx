## The brief

Opera Group builds across Navsari, in Gujarat, and the name carries weight locally after nearly three decades under the Sangani Group. Online, none of that came through. Someone who had heard about a project had nowhere to go that matched the buildings going up on the ground.

<Lede>How do you make a builder feel as solid on a screen as they do at the site office?</Lede>

A home here is the biggest decision most families make, and the site had to earn that trust before anyone picked up the phone. That framing decided the rest of it. Not a listings portal and not a brochure, but somewhere that answers the questions a buyer is already carrying around.

## Designing for the decision

The projects go first. Each one leads with a full render, then the details a buyer actually weighs: the layout, the size, and the handover date. Those sit where people look for them instead of inside a brochure that has to be downloaded first, so a buyer can rule a project in or out without asking anyone.

Enquiries open a WhatsApp thread rather than a form. That's already where these conversations happen here, and a form nobody checks is worse than no form at all. "Enquire now" and "Book a site visit" hand the sales team a live chat in the tool they're in all day.

Almost everyone arrives from a phone, so I drew those screens first and let desktop follow. Deciding the hierarchy at the narrow width meant nothing important survived just because there was room for it.

A warm, serif-led identity carries the rest, closer to a brand than a property listing.

## The full build

A case study usually shows the highlight reel. This is the Figma canvas behind it: every page and state I drew for Opera. Home variations, the project listing and detail, the location map, the about pages, all of it on desktop and mobile. Working in one canvas is what kept the type and spacing consistent from screen to screen, so the site still feels like one place once you start clicking around.

<Full src="work/opera-group/08-figma-wireframes.webp" alt="Figma canvas of every Opera Group screen: home variations, project listing and detail, location map, and about pages, across desktop and mobile" />

## How it landed

The site is live at operagroup.co.in. The sales team has something that matches how they talk about the company in person, and a buyer can size up Opera before the first call rather than after it.

This was a change of pace from the product work I usually do. No dashboards, no roles, just one job: make a real estate company feel real on a screen. The part I'd revisit is construction progress. It's what buyers worry about most and the site only hints at it, so it's still the thing that sends someone to the phone.
